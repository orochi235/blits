blits · design

An effects schema, and the API around it

A small runtime package for concurrent effects. Each effect runs on its own clock with its own state and weight, and a mix folds them into one value per subject per frame by rules that belong to the channel, not the effect.

For: whoever builds blits, and whoever ports klieg, wod, sherpa or magicsmoke onto it. Answers: what the vocabulary is, what an author writes, what a host calls, and where a second engine would plug in. The package implements this page; where the two differ, the code in src/ is the one that runs.

Built · v0.7.0 · design 2026-09-15, package 2026-09-28

In one picture

A host syncs the mix once a frame. For each subject it wants drawn, the mix probes every voice that reaches that subject, scales each delta toward its channel's rest by the voice's current weight, merges them channel by channel, and hands back one pose. The host writes that pose wherever it goes: a three.js mesh, an SVG wedge, a CSS transform, an audio gain.

Everything klieg does today fits this picture, in three copies with three channel sets. wod's transition voices are the same picture with one subject type and a retargeting tween. sherpa's seam is two voices. magicsmoke's fault is one voice whose weight is a signal.

Vocabulary

Every other name in the package is built from these.

Delta
A partial record of channel values. A patch writes only the channels it drives; an absent channel means no contribution.
Channel
One field of a delta, with its own arithmetic: how two contributions merge, what rest is if there is one, how to scale toward rest, how to lerp.
Kit
The channel set for one kind of delta. klieg's pose, klieg's part, wod's presence are each a kit.
Subject
What is being driven: a letter, a part, a wedge, a page, or a unit value for a system with no subject dimension such as klieg's lighting.
Patch
A pure function of phase and a subject that returns a delta. Optionally stateful. This is klieg's word and it keeps it.
Voice
A patch plus its own playback: start, rate, loop, weight envelope, target, and blend options. The unit a host cues and fades.
Mix
The set of live voices over one kit, synced once a frame and probed per subject. What a host holds.
Signal
A 0..1 scalar resolved per subject per frame from something outside the clock: the cursor, a hover value, another voice's state.
Handle
What cue returns: the live controls on one voice, and its completion.
Engine
The implementation behind a mix. One ships. The seam is the mix's own interface.
Pose
What one subject looks like at one frame, once every voice has been folded in. Where a delta is partial, a pose is complete: every channel of the kit is filled. This is what a host writes.
Contribution
What one voice does to one subject in one frame, once its weight is applied. Not a share of the whole: two voices each contributing gain: 0.1 leave the subject at 0.01.
Duration
How long one pass of a patch lasts, in milliseconds, whether or not the voice loops. 0 means the patch has no passes.
Pass
One run through a patch, phase 0 to 1.
Period
The duration a looping voice repeats. Patch.period is a deprecated alias for duration.
Phase
Where a voice is within its current pass, 0 to 1, wrapping. The first thing a patch is handed, and what stagger spreads per subject.
Setting
What a patch may read and did not compute, for one subject this frame: the clock and dt the whole frame shares, the elapsed, pass and weight particular to the voice, its state, and whatever the host added.
System
One complete area of driven behavior: a kit, the subject type it drives, and the mix over them. klieg's motion, effects and lighting are three systems in one host. Two systems never share a mix, so a voice belongs to exactly one.
Source
Whatever decided a voice should exist: a score, a handler on an event, a chain handing over. There is no source type and nothing to implement — a source calls cue and gets a handle. The word is here so the vocabulary can name the seam it does not own.
Score
The source that holds a host's whole plan: what plays, over what, and when. It supplies the two things blits takes as given, the timing and the cast of subjects. klieg's is spread across handlers and a Timeline; another host's may be a document or a route table. Inside blits it is a plan of voices placed on the mix clock, which knows when each plays and never what it contributes; see Score.

Informal terms are used in design work and in the code but appear nowhere in the public API, so a consumer never has to learn them.

Lane
A channel the mix computes for every subject at once, in flat arrays indexed by a number it gives each subject, rather than subject by subject. A channel runs as a lane only while every voice writing it qualifies; otherwise it takes the general path, which folds subject by subject as the mix always has. Lanes are on by default and give the same pose; MixOptions.lanes: false turns them off.
Reach
Whether a voice acts on a subject at all. A cue's subjects or target decides it, once, the first time the mix sees the subject, and the answer is kept; a subject the voice does not reach is never asked about again. Reach is fixed for the life of the voice: a falloff that varies by frame, such as a glow around the pointer, is a weight, and every reached subject pays for it.

Channels

The arithmetic of blending is a property of the operand type, so it lives on the channel and nowhere else. Every merge, fade, crossfade, and retarget in the package is one of these four operations.

interface Channel<V> {
  /** Names the arithmetic: 'sum', 'vec(3, sum)'. Two channels of one kind fold identically. Absent, a channel
     matches only itself. */
  kind?: string;
  /** The range a numeric value means anything in. The mix clamps the folded value to it, so stacked voices and a
     retarget that carries speed stop flat at the bound. Part of the kind: mul({ bounds: [0, 1] }) is 'mul[0, 1]'. */
  bounds?: readonly [min: number, max: number];
  /** Identity. Absent means the channel has none: it replaces rather than contributes. */
  rest?: V;
  /** Fold two contributions into one. */
  merge(a: V, b: V): V;
  /** Fade toward `rest` by weight 0..1. Required when `rest` is set; absent otherwise. */
  scale?(v: V, w: number): V;
  /** Interpolate, for retargeting and for blending alternatives. */
  lerp(a: V, b: V, u: number): V;
}

type Kit<O> = { readonly [K in keyof O]-?: Channel<NonNullable<O[K]>> };

Color is a value; how it merges is a parameter. A color value is OKLab [L, a, b, coverage], and oklab(0xrrggbb) makes one at full coverage. color() averages: voices stack as a premultiplied sum, so the pose is [Σw·L, Σw·a, Σw·b, Σw], which lanes and pull fold as any vec(4, sum()). blits knows no subject's own color, so the host lays the result over its base at write: toHex(pose, under) divides the coverage out, caps it at 1, mixes over under in OKLab and clips to sRGB per channel, and css(pose) writes an oklab() with the coverage as alpha. color(last()) replaces, the last voice to pass winning behind the band. mul and max per OKLab axis mean nothing anyone asks for, so color refuses them; a tint, a hue shift, chroma and lightness are channels of their own. OKLab is the store because voices average per axis there, and OKLCH's hue wraps. How two colors interpolate is the channel's choice, { lerp: 'oklab' | 'oklch' }: OKLab, the default, crosses opposite hues through a pale gray, and OKLCH goes round the hue as mixHex does, at the cost of leaving the lanes.

A channel with no rest cannot fade. The mix handles that in one way: below a weight threshold the contribution is dropped, above it the value passes through whole. That is the rule hinge's blend mode already applies to color, and it is stated here once so no consumer rediscovers it. The threshold is a band, not an edge — it drops at off and passes at on, and between the two it holds whatever it last did. One edge chatters: a weight resting on it is carried back and forth by frame noise, and every crossing drops or restores the whole contribution. Retargeting is the way a rest-less channel does move smoothly, since a lerp from a sampled value needs no identity.

The stock set is not the limit: a consumer that needs arithmetic it lacks writes its own Channel, and nothing in the mix knows the difference. sherpa's transform is one, composed by the host from whatever it puts in a matrix.

A kit is plain data, so a consumer declares one in a line and the mix is typed by it:

const PART = kit<PartOffset>({
  gain: mul(), scale: mul(), dark: max(), crawl: sum(),
  position: vec(3, sum()), rotation: vec(3, sum()),
  light: vec(3, sum()), color: color(last(), { lerp: 'oklch' }),
});

Patches

A patch is what an author writes. It is klieg's EffectPiece with three additions: the setting carries absolute time as well as normalized, a patch may declare state, and a patch declares which channels it contributes to.

interface Patch<I, O, S = void, H = unknown> {
  /** Which authoring form built it: `'fn'` from `patch`, `'keys'` from `keys`, `'motion'` from `spring`,
     `glide` or `tween`. An engine declares which forms it runs. */
  readonly form: 'fn' | 'keys' | 'motion';
  /** Milliseconds one pass lasts, whether or not the voice loops. 0 has no passes: `phase` and `pass`
     stay 0, `loop` is ignored, and the patch reads `setting.elapsed` instead. */
  duration: number;
  /** @deprecated Use `duration`. A patch that sets only `period` still plays. */
  readonly period?: number;
  /** The channels this patch contributes to. Every key `at` sets, and no others. */
  readonly writes: readonly (keyof O)[];
  /** The channels it was written against. `cue` refuses a kit whose channel of the same name is another kind. */
  readonly kit?: Partial<Kit<O>>;
  /** The fields of `setting.host` it reads. `cue` refuses a mix whose host lacks one. */
  readonly reads?: readonly string[];
  /** `phase` is 0..1 across one pass, wrapping. Not asked for a subject its voice gives no weight, unless the
     voice is fading to rest; `step` still runs. A `keys` patch's stops are not read there either, unless its
     voice is in a locus. */
  at(phase: number, subject: I, setting: Setting<S, H>): Partial<O>;

  /** Per-subject state, created on the first frame this patch sees a subject. */
  state?(subject: I): S;
  /** Advances state once per subject per probed frame, before that frame's first `at`, by the whole gap
     since that subject last advanced. `dt` is Infinity under reduced motion. */
  step?(state: S, dt: number, subject: I, setting: Setting<S, H>): void;

  /** Present when the patch was authored as keyframes, so an engine that reads data can. */
  readonly keys?: readonly Keyframe<O>[];
  /** Present on a `'motion'` patch: its kind and constants, for an engine that reads data. */
  readonly motion?: MotionSpec;
  /** A copy of `state` sharing nothing with it, for a read at another time. Default `structuredClone`. */
  clone?(state: S): S;
}

interface Setting<S = void, H = unknown> {
  /** The mix clock at this frame. Identical for every sample in the frame. */
  timestamp: number;
  /** The gap this subject is catching up by: milliseconds since it last advanced, which is the gap since
     the previous sync only for a subject probed every frame. Infinity under reduced motion: snap, never
     integrate. */
  dt: number;
  /** Milliseconds this voice has been playing, rate applied. What lets a patch see across passes. */
  elapsed: number;
  /** Which pass this is, 0 first. */
  pass: number;
  /** This voice's weight for this subject this frame, after its fade envelope, its signal and its share of any locus:
     the same number the mix is about to scale by. */
  weight: number;
  /** This subject's state, when the patch declares one. */
  state: S;
  /** Anything the host adds for its own patches: klieg's pointer and pointerInWord go here. Typed as the mix's `H`. */
  host: H;
  /** The state `owner` keeps for this voice and subject, made by `init` on first ask. Held by the mix, so a read at
     another time can copy it; a stateful signal keeps its state here and nowhere else. */
  keep<K>(owner: object, init: () => K): K;
  /** Reports an event for this voice and subject at `timestamp`. Queued until the host drains it; the mix
     never calls back. Meant for `step`. */
  send(event: unknown): void;
}

Three authoring forms build a patch. All return the same interface, so a voice cannot tell them apart.

formsignaturewhat it is
fnpatch(duration, at, { writes, state?, step? })Procedural. Flicker, roving, a hash walk, a starter strike. Only a JS engine can run one.
keyskeys(duration, stops, { ease?, easeBy?, delayBy? })Declarative stops, klieg's transition sugar and wod's keyframes. Data preserved on patch.keys for an engine that reads data instead of calling functions.
motionspring(writes, opts), glide(writes, opts), tween(writes, opts)Momentum, or an eased curve between two values, solved in closed form per subject; below. The kind and its constants are preserved on patch.motion, a MotionSpec.

writes is the patch's half of the contract the rest of the package already states: a channel kit declares what a consumer has, an engine declares its runs set, and both are checked at cue. Without it the only way to learn what a patch touches is to call it, per subject, per frame. With it the mix refuses a patch contributing a channel the kit lacks, resolves each voice's channels once at cue instead of walking the whole kit per probe, and can answer what a voice affects without probing it. A keys patch derives it from its stops.

Two small names the page leans on: Easing is a curve on 0..1, given as data where it can be — CSS’s names ('ease-in-out'), { bezier: [x1, y1, x2, y2] } or { steps, jump? }, which a WAAPI or GPU engine can read — or as a (u: number) => number, which only the CPU engine runs, and Keyframe<O> is one stop, { at: number; delta: Partial<O>; ease?: Easing }, kept on patch.keys so an engine can read what a keys patch is made of. hex, deprecated for color(last(), { lerp: 'oklch' }), holds 0xrrggbb, and mixHex is its lerp, which blends in OKLCH with hue taken the short way round, so a crossfade between hues keeps its lightness and saturation; a mix outside sRGB’s gamut is clipped per channel. hex({ space: 'srgb' }) blends red, green and blue each on its own instead, as klieg’s light channel does.

Wrappers stay wrappers. klieg's turns, roving, intermittent, hinge and partition take a patch and return a patch, and none of them changes except to pass writes through — a wrapper reshapes one contribution and never adds a channel to it. Composition at the patch level is for reshaping one contribution; composition at the mix level is for running several. The two do not merge.

Voices

A voice is a patch given a clock and a volume knob. This is the unit that answers "independent state and timeframe": two voices of the same patch are two clocks, two states, two weights.

interface VoiceSpec<I, O, H = unknown> {
  patch: Patch<I, O, any, H>;
  /** Which subjects this voice reaches. Default: all of them. The predicate is fixed at `cue`; it runs per
     subject the first time the mix sees that subject, and the answer is kept for as long as the state is. */
  target?: (subject: I) => boolean;
  /** The subjects this voice reaches, fixed at `cue` and matched by identity; a probe of any other never
     asks this voice. Takes the place of target, and a cue giving both is refused: a predicate over a
     fixed list is that list filtered, which the host can do before cueing. */
  subjects?: readonly I[];

  // clock
  /** When the voice starts, in ms on the clock the host passes sync. Default: the next sync. */
  start?: number;
  /** Playback rate. 1 is real time; 0 freezes. */
  rate?: number;
  /** true loops for good, false plays one pass, n plays n passes. A finite loop leaves when its passes are done — over fade.out where one is set, at once where none is — unless it holds after. */
  loop?: boolean | number;
  /** Per-subject delay in voice ms. klieg's stagger grammar stays klieg's and passes in the one
     thing the mix needs: how long this subject waits. Asked once per subject on first sight and
     kept, like target; the subject's fade in starts when the voice clock reaches it. */
  stagger?: (subject: I) => number;
  /** Show the first frame before a subject starts, the last after its passes run out, or both. Default: neither,
     so the voice shows nothing outside its passes. See Freezing. hold, its name before 0.6.0, still works and is deprecated. */
  freeze?: 'before' | 'after' | 'both';

  // weight
  /** Steady weight, or a signal read per subject per frame. Default 1. */
  weight?: number | Signal<I, H>;
  /** Ramp in and out, ms. Out applies on `fade()` and when a finite loop ends. */
  fade?: { in?: number; out?: number; ease?: Easing };
  /** Voices sharing a locus are alternatives: the mix folds them into one contribution through each
     channel's `lerp` before anything else sees them. A crossfade between two behaviors, not what
     klieg's phases are: those layer. */
  locus?: string;

  // blend
  /** A `keys` patch whose first stop reads "wherever this subject is now", lerped over the pass. Read from
     the pose of the frame before this voice first contributes, so it never reads itself. With no
     such frame on record it is this frame's pose with every other voice folded in. */
  from?: 'current';
  /** Words a source attaches to its voices, so their events and handles can be taken as a set. */
  tags?: readonly string[];
  // score
  /** What other voices call this one by. A label blits never reads. */
  name?: string;
  /** The plan it belongs to; a bare name in an anchor means a voice in the same score. */
  score?: string;
  /** start or in, and out or end: each a timestamp or an anchor to another voice's mark. See Score. */
  anchor?: Placement;
  /** Events at times on this voice's clock, once per pass, for `book` to hand the host ahead of time. See Time. */
  hits?: readonly { at: number; event: unknown }[];
  /** The owner it plays under, which places and times it on the owner's clock. See Score. */
  owner?: Handle<I>;
}

interface Handle<I> {
  readonly id: number;
  /** frozen: a voice freezing after has played every pass and shows its last frame until faded. */
  readonly state: 'pending' | 'live' | 'frozen' | 'fading' | 'done';
  /** The owner's handle, for a voice an owner holds. */
  readonly owner: Handle<I> | undefined;
  /** Live. Writes land on the next sync. */
  weight: number;
  rate: number;
  /** Moves this voice's clock, forward or back, and an owner's children with it. Each subject's state is made
     fresh and stepped from the voice's start to `elapsed` at its next probe, unless `state: 'keep'` leaves it
     where it was. Answers how sure that leaves it, as `assess` does. Mix time is not addressable this way,
     and nothing outside this voice moves. */
  seek(elapsed: number, opts?: { state?: 'rebuild' | 'keep' }): 'exact' | 'stepped' | 'held';
  /** Moves the rate to `rate` linearly over `over` mix ms. The voice clock integrates it, so speed eases too. */
  ramp(rate: number, over: number): void;
  /** Ramps out over `fade.out` unless given a fade here; `at: 'rest'` waits per subject for a resting pose first.
     Given a `subject`, fades that subject alone out of this voice, which plays on for the rest. */
  fade(opts?: { over?: number; at?: 'rest'; deadline?: number } | { subject: I; over?: number }): void;
  /** The weight this voice gave `subject` the last frame it was probed: after fades and its signal, before a
     locus folds it. 0 where it gave none. One number on a record the mix keeps anyway, so free unasked. */
  weightOf(subject: I): number;
  /** Resolves when the voice has been removed from the mix, however that happened. */
  readonly done: Promise<void>;
  /** Resolves true when a finite loop's last pass ends, for the latest-staggered subject it has seen, and false
     if the voice leaves before that. Never rejects. */
  readonly played: Promise<boolean>;
}

Fading at a time. fade({ at, over }) with at a mix time begins the fade exactly there rather than at the call: ahead, the voice plays untouched until then, its out and end marks fixed from the call, so marks, project and anchors see them; at or behind now, the fade began then and is partway. A rise before it begins takes it back with nothing changed. It wins over an anchored out. Built 2026-10-07 for astv’s rosters, whose departures begin exactly when an arrival ends; at: 'rest' keeps its meaning.

Fading one subject. fade({ subject, over }) ramps one subject out of a voice and leaves the voice playing for every other subject; over: 0 takes it out at once. When the ramp ends the voice forgets the subject — its record, its lane position and a motion patch’s state for it — and no longer reaches it, so nothing it held stays stacked under whatever takes the subject over. A motion patch’s to brings it back, met afresh: from, target and stagger are asked again. Any other voice reaches it again only once drop forgets the subject. This is what lets a host that starts many short animations, one per node, run them as one voice on one lane instead of a voice per call, while still cancelling a node or handing it to a different patch.

Freezing. A voice shows nothing outside its passes unless it freezes, the name SMIL gives the same thing (fill="freeze"). freeze: 'before' shows a subject the voice’s first frame from the cue until the subject starts: while the voice is pending, and while the subject waits out its stagger. freeze: 'after' shows the last frame of the last pass once a finite loop has played out, and the voice stays until fade() takes it out, ramping from that frame. 'both' does both. wod asked for it to replace WAAPI’s fill: a spin lands and freezes on its angle, and staggered wedges wait on their first frame rather than popping in.

  • A frozen subject’s clock stands still. It reads phase 0 of pass 0 before, and phase 1 of the last pass after, with elapsed pinned to that edge. at is asked for that frame. step does not run while a subject waits, so a stateful patch starts playing from the state it was cued with; after, it runs once more to where the last pass ended and then no more, so the frozen frame does not depend on when the last frame before it fell. A voice freezing before stays pending until it starts, and weightOf reports the weight it gives a waiting subject.
  • fade.in counts from the first frame the voice shows, so with 'before' it ramps in on the frozen frame rather than at full weight, and does not dip when play begins.
  • A voice freezing after reaches frozen, not fading, at the moment a voice without a freeze would begin its fade out. played resolves true then. Its out and end marks are unfixed until it is faded, like those of a voice that loops for good, so { after: 'spin' } waits for the fade. Something that should start when the passes run out anchors to its coast mark, or awaits played.
  • The host owns every frozen voice. One cued per spin and never faded stays in the mix for good, one more voice folded every frame. The next voice’s cue is the time to fade the last, at once or at: 'rest'.
  • Where it changes nothing. A voice that loops for good never runs out of passes, so only 'before' applies to it. A motion patch (spring, glide, tween) already keeps its target and has no passes, so freeze does nothing to it. Lanes give the same pose with it as without them, as for everything else.
  • What it costs. A voice freezing before runs on the general path while it is pending, which takes the channels it writes off their lanes until it starts; one that is already live, waiting only on a subject’s stagger, stays on its lane. A mix with no freeze reads no slower (keys and fn rows of bench/frame.mjs, three passes alternated against main on a laptop, 2026-10-03).

Weights are the only knob the mix owns. Fade, crossfade, handover, alternatives, and hinge's continuous mode are all weights over time; the section on blending shows each one as such. A patch never sees a weight applied to its output, it only sees the number in setting.weight if it cares.

Mix

What a host holds. The methods are few because the voices carry the options.

function mix<I, O, H = unknown>(kit: Kit<O>, opts?: MixOptions<H>): Mix<I, O, H>;

interface MixOptions<H = unknown> {
  engine?: Engine;                // default: mixer
  /** Reduced motion: fades and warm-ups snap, `dt` reads Infinity. Read once at creation, or a signal. */
  reduce?: boolean | (() => boolean);
  /** Merged into every `setting.host`. `H` types it for every patch and signal the mix cues, so none needs a cast. */
  host?: H;
  /** The band a rest-less channel's contribution switches across. Default 0.4 / 0.6, a placeholder. */
  band?: { on: number; off: number };
  /** The most `dt` a step or signal is handed, however long the gap. Off by default. */
  maxDt?: number;
  /** Run every `step` once per interval of this many ms instead of once by the frame's gap. Off by default. */
  stepMs?: number;
  /** How far back `project` may read, and how often a stateful voice's state is copied on the way. Off by default. */
  history?: { ms: number; every?: number; inputs?: boolean; tape?: TapeMaker; store?: HistoryStore;
             revive?(d: Described): VoiceSpec };
  /** A subject's key for a history `store`, where subjects are objects. */
  keyOf?(subject: I): string | number;
  /** Whether a channel may run as a lane. On by default; the pose is the same either way, apart from
     a stateless patch's `send` from `at` and a patch that starts keeping state partway through. */
  lanes?: boolean;
}

interface Mix<I, O, H = unknown> {
  /** Cues a voice. Throws when the engine cannot run the patch's form, or the kit lacks a channel it `writes`. */
  cue(spec: VoiceSpec<I, O, H>): Handle<I>;
  /** N voices whose weights split one signal, k in 0..1, cued into one locus so they fold as
     alternatives. Two patches crossfade; more make stops. The signal is read once per subject per
     frame, and every member takes its share of that reading. */
  blend(patches: readonly Patch<I, O, any, H>[], by: Signal<I, H>, spec?: Omit<VoiceSpec<I, O, H>, 'patch' | 'weight'>): Handle<I>[];
  /** An owner: a voice with no patch that plays the voices cued with `owner: handle` on its own clock. See Score. */
  owns(spec: OwnerSpec<I, H>): Handle<I>;

  /** The host reports the clock, once a frame. Nothing advances at the call: it publishes the reading, and
     each subject catches up by the whole gap the first time it is probed afterward. The same timestamp twice
     changes nothing, and an earlier one throws short of a `rebase`: `seek` moves the mix. */
  sync(timestamp: number): void;
  /** The time between the last sync and the next one does not count. Called when a hidden tab comes back. */
  rebase(): void;
  /** The mix's own rate, multiplied into every voice's; 0 pauses the whole mix. See Time. */
  rate: number;
  /** Moves the mix's rate to `rate` linearly over `over` ms of the host's clock. */
  ramp(rate: number, over: number): void;
  /** The merged pose for one subject at the synced frame. `out` is written in place when given. */
  probe(subject: I, out?: O): O;
  /** Each subject's pose written into one Float64Array per channel, in the order given: what probe(subject, out)
     gives, without a pose object per subject. A channel of n numbers takes n places a subject. */
  pull(subjects: Iterable<I>, into: { [K in numeric channels of O]?: Float64Array }): void;
  /** Every channel at rest for this subject this frame, so a host can skip the write. */
  atRest(subject: I): boolean;
  /** The mix clock at the last sync or seek, which `seek` and `project` take; NaN before the first sync. */
  readonly now: number;
  /** The mix read at another mix time without moving it: ahead plays what is cued forward, behind needs `history`. */
  project(time: number): Projection<I, O>;
  /** Moves the mix to a mix time under `history` with a `tape`, and plays on from there, making the host's
     recorded calls again as it passes them. See Score. */
  seek(time: number): void;
  /** Loads from the history `store` what a seek or read to `time` needs, where that is past memory. */
  prepare(time: number): Promise<void>;
  /** Where the mix records the host's calls: weasel-history's `History`, made by `history.tape`. */
  readonly tape: Tape | undefined;
  /** Puts a named mark on a score, now or at a timestamp, for anchors to target. */
  announce(name: string, opts?: { at?: number; score?: string; tags?: readonly string[] }): void;
  /** Every mark the plan knows between two timestamps, earliest first. */
  marks(from: number, to: number): Marked[];

  /** A handle on every voice still in the mix, pending, live, frozen or fading, in cue order; given a tag, only
     the voices carrying it. Each is the handle `cue` returned for that voice. */
  voices(tag?: string): Handle<I>[];

  /** Anything still contributing, fading, or pending. A host stops its loop when this is false. `cue` and
     `blend` make it true again at once, and `onWake` says so. */
  readonly live: boolean;
  /** Another frame would change no pose: every voice is done, frozen at a plain weight, or a motion whose every
     subject has landed, or, at rate 0, every voice has started at a plain weight with no anchor. A host may sleep its loop while this is true and `live` still is. */
  readonly inert: boolean;
  /** Calls `fn` when a host call makes the mix need frames again after a sync: a cue, a fade, a rate, a seek, a
     `drop`, a motion retargeted. Once until the next sync, and never during one: the only time the mix calls
     back into the host, and always from inside a call the host made. A sleeping loop wakes on it. */
  onWake(fn: () => void): () => void;
  /** Fades every voice out: over `over` when given, over each voice's own `fade.out` otherwise. */
  mute(opts?: { over?: number }): void;
  /** Forgets per-subject state, a motion patch's included. Call when a subject is retired, or let the WeakMap do it. */
  drop(subject: I): void;
  /** Every event sent since the last drain, earliest first. Drain after probing. */
  drain<E>(tag?: string): { timestamp: number; subject: I; voice: number; tags: readonly string[]; event: E }[];
  /** Hands the host each mark and hit `ahead` ms before it comes, on a clock of its own, and stops what moves. See Time. */
  book<E>(opts: { clock: () => number; ahead: number; late: number; tag?: string; score?: string;
    take(item: Marked | BookedHit<E>, when: number, lateBy: number): { stop(): void } | undefined }): { stop(): void };
}

A probe walks the voices that may reach its subject, not every voice. With no target in the mix that is all of them, so nothing is kept; once a voice targets, each subject keeps the list of voices whose target took it, rebuilt when a voice is cued, starts or leaves. One voice per subject on a shared mix then costs one voice per probe: 1000 subjects, each with its own voice, took 95 ms a frame before the list and 0.45 ms after (npm run bench, rows own). Building the list still asks every target of every new subject, so the first frame of that scene took 500–580 ms. A voice that names its subjects with subjects is filed under each of them at cue, and a subject's list is built from the voices filed under it and those that name none: the same scene's first frame took 1.0 ms, and 10.6 ms at 10,000 subjects (rows named).

Subjects are not registered. State lives in a WeakMap keyed by the subject object, created on first probe and stepped by the gap in the timestamp since that subject was last stepped. That is the rule klieg's dwell arrived at, and it means a part a wrapper leaves unasked for a while catches up rather than resuming where it paused.

Signals

A signal is a 0..1 scalar resolved per subject per frame. It is the package's one input type from outside the clock, and it plugs in at exactly two places: a voice's weight, and blend's selector.

type Signal<I, H = unknown> = ((subject: I, setting: Setting<void, H>) => number) & { readonly input?: boolean };

// stock combinators; klieg's own `near`, `dwell`, `level` are consumers' signals built on these
function peak<I>(...signals: Signal<I>[]): Signal<I>;          // max
function slew<I>(of: Signal<I>, { riseMs, fallMs, from }): Signal<I>;  // stateful: rate-limited follow toward its input, from `from` or its input
function lag<I>(of: Signal<I>, { riseMs, fallMs, floor, from }): Signal<I>;  // stateful: exponential follow, time constants
function level<I>(initial?: number): Signal<I> & { set(v: number): void };  // host-written
function gate<I>(of: Signal<I>, band: { on: number; off: number }): Signal<I>;  // stateful: holds inside the band
function gate<I>(of: Signal<I>, at: number): Signal<I>;          // sugar for on = off = at

A stateful signal keeps its state in the mix, through setting.keep(owner, init): per voice and per subject, keyed by the signal itself, made on first ask. So two voices handed the same slew each follow on their own, except a blend’s members, which read their signal once and keep its state once between them; and a read at another time can copy what the mix holds instead of moving it. A signal measures its own gap, from the timestamp it last saw, rather than the gap its voice caught up by. input marks a signal that reads something the clock does not drive: level sets it, and peak, slew, gate and blend’s shares inherit it from what they read. slew is klieg's dwell with the cursor factored out; gate keeps one bit, which way it last switched.

slew and lag are the package's two decay primitives. slew follows its input at a rate limit, no faster than riseMs per unit climbing or fallMs draining; it stays linear, because klieg's three decays are and the port's baselines depend on it. lag closes 1 − exp(−gap / ms) of the distance per read, so it reads the same at any frame spacing while its input holds still; it lands on its input within floor (1e-6), because an exponential never reaches rest and atRest needs it to, and floor: 0 keeps the curve exact. magicsmoke's intensity ease is a lag. It is not Easing, which is a curve over a known duration, and it is not called ease for that reason — fade.ease and keys({ ease }) already mean the curve. A decay tail is a slew to 0 with no rise; a fade is a slew on a weight; klieg's kicks is a slew to 0 that peak pokes back up. Nothing else in the package stores a value in order to follow it. gate is the other half of that pair and not a substitute for it: a slew sets how fast a value may move, a band sets whether it may move at all. A slew cannot quiet a gate, because a rate limit delays a crossing without deciding it, and an input resting on the threshold drags the slewed value onto the threshold with it.

Time

  • The mix is pull-based on a host clock. The host owns the frame loop and calls sync(timestamp) with milliseconds on any monotonic clock it likes. klieg passes its Clock.now(); wod passes performance.now(); a test passes integers. A host with no loop of its own can take ticker()'s: it syncs every mix it was given on each animation frame, or on a timer where there are none, as in a worker, sleeps while every mix is inert, wakes on onWake, and caps the rate at fps when asked.
  • Everything in a frame reads one timestamp. A second sync with the same value is a no-op, and every probe between syncs answers from the same frame. This is what makes state advance once however many probes a wrapper makes.
  • A voice's clock is (timestamp − start) · rate. That reading is setting.elapsed, and its position within the current pass, (elapsed mod duration) / duration, is the phase the patch is handed first. Both reach the patch. A rate change rebases so elapsed is continuous.
  • The mix has a rate of its own, mix.rate and mix.ramp(rate, over), shaped like a handle's and multiplied into every voice's: 0 pauses the whole mix, and a negative rate throws: the mix plays only forward, and seek moves it back. It runs the mix clock: mix time is the host's timestamp, less what rebase took out, integrated at the mix's rate, and each voice integrates its own rate over mix time, so a ramp on both multiplies and no position jumps. Everything a voice owns runs on mix time and slows with it, which is what a pause has to mean for a fade caught halfway. A timestamp the host gives or reads stays on the host's clock, so a beat announced from an audio clock stays on the beat when the mix slows, and a start ahead of a paused mix waits for the host's clock to reach it. The mix's own ramp runs over host ms, since a ramp over its own slowing time would never arrive. A change made while paused lands at the next sync as always, and a weight signal is still asked at every probe, so one reading input follows it. At rate 0 the mix is inert once every voice not done has started, at a plain weight, with no anchor. Under history every rate change is kept, and a read back finds mix time through them as it finds a voice's clock through its handle's changes; without it a timestamp from before the last change converts at the rate in force, as one from before a rebase converts at the offset in force. weasel also scales a key and a handle; a mix leaves those levels to tags and to the host.

  • dt is derived, never supplied. The gap between this sync and the last, per subject, and Infinity under reduced motion. A patch that integrates guards against that with a snap, as klieg's already do.
  • Time away is the host's to call. A hidden tab gets no frames, so its next sync can be an hour past the last. By default that hour is a gap like any other: fades and finite loops finish, and every subject catches up by all of it. A host that calls mix.rebase() on visibilitychange says the gap does not count, and the mix clock — what setting.timestamp reads, and what voices, fades and subjects run on — resumes where it was left. It is then the host's timestamp less the time taken out, and a start the host gives is read on the host's clock and converted. maxDt caps the dt a step or signal is handed for any gap, the net an integrator wants; it is off by default because a patch with a closed form wants the whole gap.
  • There is no frame rate, only an optional step interval. A frame is a reading, so a stateless patch reads the same at any rate. A step handed the frame's gap does not: an integrator gives a different answer at 30 and 144 fps. stepMs runs step once per whole interval instead, on a grid counted from when the subject's delay ran out, carrying the remainder, so the answer and every event's time are the same at any frame rate. maxDt then caps how many intervals one sample runs. Signals and at still read the frame. The host's clock is whatever it passes sync: a rAF timestamp and performance.now() share an origin, and Date.now() does not.
  • Events come out by draining. A patch calls setting.send(event), usually from step, and the host calls mix.drain() after probing to take everything sent, earliest first. Events are made while a subject catches up, so a subject nobody probes has sent nothing yet, and promptness is the host's to buy by probing. Under stepMs each is stamped with the end of the interval it happened in, so the events and their times do not depend on how the probes are spaced. Time taken out by rebase sends nothing. The exception is a stateless patch sending from at on a lane: a lane fills every subject the mix has met, so it sends for each of them every frame, probed or not. Sent events are not known ahead, so they stay drain-only; what is known ahead is booked.
  • What the plan knows ahead is booked against an outside clock. A host that schedules on a clock of its own needs to hear about a time before it arrives: a sound on an AudioContext, a MIDI message sent with a timestamp, a video that has to be loaded before the voice that shows it starts. mix.book hands it two kinds of item: every mark marks lists, and a voice's hits, events at times on its own clock.

Booking runs at each sync and takes whatever falls within ahead host ms, which has to cover the longest gap between syncs. when is on the outside clock, by an offset that folds in 5% of each frame's difference and starts over from the clock's own reading on a jump past 50 ms (weasel's filter). A booking whose time moves by more than 1 ms, or that goes away, has its stop called and is booked again at the same sync: a seek, a rate or ramp on the voice, an owner or the mix, a fade or mute, a rebase, an anchor's target moving, the voice leaving. A fade takes away the hits after the voice is gone, and the hits within the fade still play. An item first seen already past, from a voice cued partway through or a stalled frame, is taken at the outside clock's now with lateBy in host ms, unless that is more than late. What a seek jumps over is not late; it is skipped. Hits come as the voice clock runs forward, so a voice run backwards books none. tag and score split one mix between bookers, and stop() on what book returns stops every booking still ahead. project books nothing, and under history nothing about booking is kept or replayed: a read back says what was planned, not what was booked. A mix with no booker pays nothing for it.
  • A finite loop ends when its last pass does, not at the frame that notices: its fade out counts from that moment, or it turns held then if it holds after, so it plays the same at any frame rate and a read at another time can find it. A fade's end and a handover's deadline are kept the same way.
  • Calls land on the next sync. A host writes handle.weight or calls fade on its own clock; the mix applies it at the first timestamp that sees it. Nothing changes mid-frame.

The four behaviors asked for, each as the weight or lerp it reduces to. Nothing here is a mechanism of its own.

A locus is folded by lerp, not by scaling and merging. Voices sharing a locus are alternatives in time, so the mix combines them into one contribution first: each member weighted by its share of the locus's summed weight, through the channel's own lerp, in cue order. That one contribution then contributes at weight min(1, Σw) and is scaled toward rest like any other. On a sum channel this is exactly w₁a + w₂b, the arithmetic klieg's timeline already runs, so the cap it was written as is a special case of the fold rather than a rule of its own. On mul, max and last it is the difference between an alternative and a pile-up: two voices halfway through a handover, both holding a gain of 0.06, fold to 0.06 — which is what one alternative means — where scaling and merging reads 0.28, the product of two half-scaled copies. A grouped voice may write only channels that have a lerp, which every stock channel does. Cue order is visible only to a lerp that is not linear, such as a hue taking the short way round.

the one rule

A weight scales a contribution toward its channel's rest. A lerp moves between two values. Every blend is one or the other, chosen by the channel. A consumer that needs a third arithmetic adds a channel, not a mix option.

Springs, glides and tweens

Motion that has momentum is not a curve over a known duration, so it is neither keys nor Easing. Two patches carry it, both of form motion, each solved in closed form per subject, so where a subject is at a given moment does not depend on how the frames that sample it were spaced. Each keeps one stretch per subject: released at a voice time from a position and a velocity, heading somewhere. A change mid-flight ends the stretch and starts the next from where the subject is and how fast it is moving, which is what keeps a retarget from kinking.

const s = spring<Node, Pose>('x', { from: 0, to: 100, stiffness: 170, damping: 26 });
mix.cue({ patch: s });
s.to(node, 40);              // heads elsewhere from wherever it is, keeping its velocity
s.push(node, 600);           // a kick: same target, new velocity
s.read(node);                // { value, velocity }, for handing the motion to another patch

const g = glide<Node, Pose>('x', { from: 0, velocity: 1200, ms: 325 });  // a fling: rests 390 past the start

const t = tween<Node, Pose>('x', { from: (n) => n.x, to: (n) => n.goal, ms: 400, ease: 'ease-out' });
t.to(node, 40);              // from wherever it is, over a full 400 ms again

A tween has no momentum: it is an easing over ms from one value to another. It is a motion patch rather than a keys one because its endpoints are each subject's own data, read once when the mix first meets the subject, and it retargets one subject at a time like a spring. A retarget starts from where the subject is and runs the easing again from 0, so its velocity jumps where a spring's would not. ms may be a function of the subject, asked each time one of its stretches starts, so a retarget can take its own duration. A voice's loop and period do not repeat it. It takes the easings keys takes; its velocity, which read reports, is the easing's slope, which a lane never computes, since a mix only wants the value.

  • Time is the voice's, so rate and seek apply. A change takes effect at the voice time it names, or, named none, at the subject's own voice time at the mix's latest frame, whether or not the host probed it then; naming one is what makes a retarget land the same at any frame rate. A subject no frame of its voice has met yet, or still inside its stagger, takes an unnamed change at its first read, after any named change due by then. read with no time answers at that same latest frame, counting every change due by then, and answers nothing once the voice is gone.
  • History is kept to the mix's horizon. Each subject keeps every stretch a read may still reach, so a read earlier than a change finds the stretch that was playing then. A mix without history keeps only the stretch playing now.
  • A spring or a glide settles. Within settle (1e-4) of where it heads, and that slow, it lands there exactly, so atRest sees it. settle: 0 keeps the curve exact. A tween needs none: it lands on its target exactly at ms.
  • The state is the patch's own, per subject, rather than the mix's, because the host retargets through the patch. Cue each one on one voice. A patch moves every subject on the same number of axes, and throws on a subject, a to or a push with another, leaving the subject as it was. Its values are the kind its channel holds, a number for a number and an array for an array: one every subject shares is refused at cue, one given per subject on that subject's first sample.
  • The constants are data. patch.motion, a MotionSpec, is { kind: 'spring', stiffness, damping, mass, settle } or { kind: 'glide', ms, settle } or { kind: 'tween', ms, ease }, with ms undefined where it varies by subject, so an engine can run one without calling at. Where each subject starts and heads stays on the patch, since either may be a function of the subject.
  • Handing motion between patches is read on the old one and from plus velocity on the new: a fling that ends in a spring starts the spring at the glide's velocity.

Score

The score is the plan: when each voice plays, for how long, and relative to which other voice. It never says what a voice contributes, which stays in the patch and the channels, and it never reads a channel's value. A voice has five marks on the mix clock: start (its fade in begins), in (full weight), coast (its last pass ends), out (its fade out begins) and end (gone). A voice coasts when played resolves true, for its latest-staggered subject: with its out where it does not freeze, and where it starts showing its last frame where it does, so { of: 'run', mark: 'coast' } waits on a frozen voice that { after: 'run' } would wait on until it is faded. An owner coasts with its last child, a span where its fit ends. A voice that loops for good, or fades before its last pass ends, never coasts. Named 2026-10-07.

  • Placing. A cue anchors at most one of start and in, and one of out and end, to a timestamp or to another voice's mark: anchor: { in: 12_400 } is fully in by 12.4 s, anchor: { start: { after: 'intro' } } starts once intro is gone. Anchoring in or end counts the voice's own fade. A query selects by name, tag or a channel the voice writes, and its resolver picks among several, last cued by default.
  • Several scores. Each source keeps its own plan: a voice's score names it, and the mix plays every score it holds at once, the way a DAW plays every track. Names are namespaced by score, so a bare name in an anchor means a voice in the asker's score and a query names score to reach into another. A voice with none is in the mix's one unnamed score.
  • Waiting and following. An anchor with no answer — a name not cued, the out of a voice that loops for good — leaves the voice pending. While it waits it follows its target, so a target faded early brings it forward; once it starts, its start is fixed. An out or end follows until the fade begins. A target that has left keeps the time it last gave, and can be anchored to fresh only while the mix's history reaches it. An anchored mark already past starts the voice partway through. A placement that waits on itself is refused at cue.
  • Joins. { all: [...] } waits on several anchors and answers the latest of them, once every one has answered: { start: { all: [{ after: 'change-3' }, { of: 'orb-7', mark: 'end' }] } } starts once both are done. { any: [...] } answers the earliest, once one of them has passed or every one has answered, since a member still unknown might yet answer earlier. A join holds only the anchors listed in it, each with its own by, and nests. A member whose target has left keeps the time it last gave, as a lone anchor does, so one target finishing early no longer leaves the whole join unanswered. Added 2026-10-07, named after Promise.all and Promise.any, which behave the same way.
  • Announced marks. The host puts a named mark on a score with mix.announce('reply'), or announce('beat', { at }) for one it knows ahead, and anchors target it as they target a voice's marks: a voice placed { with: 'reply' } waits pending until the reply is announced. A mark announced ahead is seen by a read ahead; one not yet announced makes a read ahead say held for whatever waits on it, and a read back sees only what had been announced by then. An announced mark stays while it is ahead or while history reaches it. Decided 2026-10-01: a mark is any named time an anchor can hang from, a voice's own four or one the host announced, and an event is only what a patch sends to the host. What fires a mark on a condition, such as a channel crossing a value, stays the host's: blits does not read values.
  • Owners. mix.owns(spec) cues an owner, a voice with no patch, and a voice cued with owner: handle plays under it, so a group can be placed, timed and faded as one. The mix's own clock is the outermost owner, and an owner is a link in the same chain: owners nest.

mix.cue({ patch: glow, owner: intro, anchor: { start: { after: 'slide' } } });

The span fits again when a child joins and when one leaves, and after a seek. A fit sees only the children that have arrived, so late ones are squeezed into what is left. A skipped child lands on its final state at that moment and its end mark is there, but the events its patch would have sent on the way are not sent. A span with a budget stays until the budget has passed, though its children finish sooner, so a late child can still join, and its end mark is wherever its children end or its budget does, whichever is later. A span held by a span is fitted through its own rate. A fit is made on the live mix and logged as the children's control changes, so a read back, a seek and its replay all see it, and the tape records nothing for it. Designed and built 2026-10-07.
  • What comes next. mix.marks(from, to) lists every mark the plan has fixed in a range, with each voice's id, name and tags. One nothing has fixed is left out.
  • Reading another time. mix.project(t) copies what each subject holds and reads it at t, sending nothing and committing nothing, so the live mix plays on exactly as if it had not been read. Ahead, it plays what is cued forward in one step across the gap. Behind, it needs history: the mix keeps every voice's control changes with when they landed, the voices that have left, and a copy of each stateful record per subject every every ms, and a read back restores the nearest copy at or before t and steps forward. A subject it kept nothing for starts from its voice's start with fresh state. A patch's own clone copies state that structuredClone cannot. With inputs, history also keeps what each input signal on a voice's weight read per subject, and a copy of the host fields patches reads, each time they changed, and a read back uses those instead of asking the signal or the host. Where anchors fixed a voice's start or out after the time read, it reads back as it stood then.
  • Seeking. mix.seek(t) moves the live mix to mix time t, as it stood at the end of that frame, and it plays on from there. It needs history with a tape, and reaches back as far as a read back does. history.tape takes weasel-history's createHistory from @weasel-js/history, or anything of the Tape shape, and the mix records on it every call the host makes on the mix, its handles and its spring and glide patches: cues, weight, rate, seeks and fades, retargets and pushes, the mix's rate, marks, mute and drop, each at the mix time it was made. The tape lives as long as the mix and is never saved.
    • Back. The mix restores itself from what history kept: each voice's controls, the voices and marks, and the mix's rate, as they stood at t. Voices that left after it are back on the handles the host holds, with done and played starting over where they had settled since. Voices cued after it wait as pending on theirs until the mix plays their cue again; on a branch that never does, they wait for good. Each stateful record is put back from the copy kept nearest before t the first time it is touched, and steps once to where it is next probed, exact under stepMs. A subject faded out of a voice or dropped after t comes back with the record it had, a motion patch’s state for it included, which history keeps for it from when it left; the lanes qualify afresh. Recorded input and host fields after t are let go, so signals and host fields read live again, and assess says held for what they feed.
    • Forward. Through a sync or a later seek, the mix makes the recorded calls again, each with the mix moved to the time it was made at, so the mix plays on as it did, frame for frame under stepMs. Ahead of anything recorded, a seek forward is a sync to there without the host's clock moving.
    • Branches. A call the host makes while recorded calls lie ahead starts a new branch, and the tape keeps the old future beside it rather than dropping it: scrubbing never loses a recording, and neither does a new call. mix.tape.branches() lists the futures at the tape's current entry and switchBranch picks one. To reach one, seek to just before the call that started the other; a call made at the same mix time as a recorded call it follows cannot be reached apart from that call.
    • The host's clock. The host goes on passing its own clock: the next sync reads t plus the host's time since its last sync, and rebase composes with it. sync refuses an earlier timestamp short of a rebase. seek, project and mix.now are mix time, since once the mix replays a recording one host time can name two moments.
    • Events. Playing past a time again sends its events and books its marks and hits again, since the host knows it went back; what was sent or booked at or before t is not sent again, and undrained events after t are dropped.
    • Reading ahead past the tape. A read ahead of the mix after a seek back throws for a time past a call the tape will make again: a recorded call is bound to the live mix and its voices, and a projection is a copy, so it would leave the call out. A seek there makes it.

The mix goes back by itself rather than through snapshots the host saves and loads, since a host-kept log of its own calls would be a second record of what the mix did. What happened after t plays again rather than vanishing, because scrubbing must never cut off a recorded future. A call made while rewound branches rather than dropping the old future, and the mix drops its own copies of state after the fork rather than keeping them per branch, so memory stays inside the horizon. History lives only for the session. The mix's own logs hold what happened up to the playhead and the tape holds the calls after it, so nothing is recorded twice and the logs never branch. A recorded call's invert does nothing, since the mix restores rather than undoing; so the mix moves its tape back only with goto, forward one redo at a time, and opens no journals.
  • How sure. assess says per channel held where a voice feeding it reads input from outside the clock with no recording of it (a signal marked input, a patch that reads host fields, or a from: 'current' start read back with nothing kept), stepped where state crossed a gap in one go without stepMs, or a stateful signal did, and exact otherwise. The least sure voice decides. Under stepMs a read back lands on the pose the mix showed, so a scrub back and forth gives the same picture. A control change the host makes between a sync and the frame's first probe or pull showed in that frame, so a read back shows it there; one made after shows from the next frame.
  • A voice's own seek. handle.seek(elapsed) moves one voice's clock, which leaves the state its patch steps and its signals keep belonging to the old position. By default each subject's state is rebuilt: made fresh and stepped from the voice's start to the new position at its next probe, on the stepMs grid where there is one, so under stepMs it lands where a voice cued fresh and played there would. state: 'keep' leaves it as it was. The seek answers how sure the state now is: exact for a voice with no state, or rebuilt under stepMs with no maxDt; stepped where it was caught up in one step, or a signal's kept state was started over; held where it was kept, or depends on host fields or an input weight a rebuild cannot know. A rebuild does not read history yet: starting from a copy kept near the new position would save the stepping, and could make state fed by recorded input exact. Decided and built 2026-10-08.
  • Cost. A projection copies each subject's record on first probe: about 3 ms for 1000 subjects under three voices, three times a live frame (npm run bench, rows ahead and back). That is cheap enough to make one per frame for a continuous scrub. History costs one control entry per handle change and one copy per stateful subject per every, all dropped past the horizon.

Transport

A transport is one clock several mixes play on, each over its own kit: astv’s orb flights, text runs and snapshot rail are mixes whose poses never fold together but whose timing does. Every mix has one; a mix made alone makes its own, so the transport is the mix clock under its own name rather than a layer on top. A host shares one by making it and passing it to each mix.

const t = transport({ history: { ms: 10_000, tape: createHistory } });
const orbs = mix(FLIGHT, { transport: t, name: 'orbs' });
const runs = mix(ROWS, { transport: t, name: 'runs' });

rail.cue({ patch: next, anchor: { start: { all: [
  { of: { score: 'step:12', tag: 'motion', resolver: 'latest' }, mark: 'coast', by: restMs },
  { with: { score: 'step:12', name: 'built' } },
] } } });
t.sync(now); // once a frame, after the frame's cues
  • One clock. t.sync, rebase, rate, ramp and seek move every mix on it; a mix on a shared transport throws for its own, naming the transport. Probing, cueing and handles stay per mix.
  • One score across mixes. A query that names a score looks in every mix on the transport, and among the marks announced there; one that names none stays in its own mix, so two windows’ bare names never meet. t.announce takes a named score. Voice ids come from the transport, so resolvers order across mixes by cue and marks and book keys stay unique; Marked.mix names the mix a mark came from.
  • Cue before sync. A pending voice’s anchor is answered again at every sync until it starts, so motion cued while it waits pushes it later. A voice cued after the sync that reached the anchor’s time is cued after the start: a host cues a frame’s voices before t.sync.
  • Reading and waking. t.marks lists every mix’s; t.project(time).of(mix) reads every mix at once, so anchors across them answer, and a mix’s own project does the same and returns its part. t.inert holds while every mix is inert, and t.onWake fires once a change in any mix needs frames.
  • Seeking. One tape records every mix’s calls. Back, each mix restores itself; forward, the tape makes the calls again in the order they were made, every mix moved to each call’s time first, so an anchor across mixes answers as it did live. A call after a seek back branches the whole transport. A mix’s history is the transport’s, and a mix given its own throws.
  • Joining and leaving. A mix joins at the transport’s now and plays a voice cued with an earlier start partway, as a mix alone does. t.drop(mix) takes one off; under history a seek back before the drop puts it back as it was and playing on drops it again, and without history it is let go.
  • What history keeps. Everything within its reach comes back exactly, or the read throws: a seek or read back older than history, and a read ahead past a call the tape will make again. ms: Infinity keeps everything. Recorded input after a seek back is let go and read live, and assess says so. A seek back moves the reach with it only as far as what was kept: a second seek further back than the first one's memory throws.
  • History behind a store. With history.store, what ms would drop is handed to store.page as plain data instead, and nothing is ever just gone: ms bounds memory, not reach. A seek or read past memory needs await t.prepare(time) first, which calls store.load(time); without it, it throws HistoryMiss, naming the mix, before anything moves. A seek back calls store.cut(time), since what was paged after it is from a future the tape makes again. load returns everything a restore needs for every mix; a store that returns all it holds is correct. Records are keyed by the mix’s name, which every mix on a transport with a store needs, the voice’s id and the subject’s key: a string or number subject is its own, and an object subject needs keyOf, or the mix throws the first time it meets one. A patch with state or step gives pack and unpack, or cue refuses it; spring, glide and tween runs are paged by blits itself. What a stateful signal keeps goes by the order its owners first kept it on the record, since a signal is code no store can hold, and the same code keeps in the same order. A voice cued with as: { kind, data } leaves memory once history no longer reaches its end, and history.revive builds it again when a seek needs it, behind the handle the host holds; one cued without as, an owner, a span, a blend member or a voice still holding a join’s answers stays in memory. With a store blits never prunes the tape, nor dropped mixes, rate changes or announced marks: the host bounds the tape. Built 2026-10-07.

Designed and built 2026-10-07 (docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-transport-design.md). Not built: owners or spans holding voices in another mix.

Patch state

klieg built three stateful patches in one week, power, kicks and dwell, and each rediscovered the same three rules: key state by the subject object, step once per frame by the gap in the timestamp, snap under infinite dt. Those rules are the package's, applied by the mix, and a patch declares only what its state is and how it advances.

const power = patch<PartInfo, PartOffset, PowerState>(0, (phase, part, setting) => {
  const s = setting.state;
  return s.mode === 'on' ? {} : { gain: s.gain };
}, {
  state: () => ({ mode: 'on', gain: 1, since: 0 }),
  step: (s, dt, part, setting) => { /* advance the strike, the flare, the warm-up */ },
});

Sign-wide state, which power has, is per-subject state on a shared subject: the patch's state returns one object from a closure and every part reads it. The mix does not need to know.

Engines

The engine is the implementation behind Mix. The schema above is data plus pure functions, so a mix can be interpreted by more than one thing. One engine ships, and the seam is designed so the second one does not need the first rewritten.

interface Engine {
  readonly name: string;
  /** Which patch forms this engine can run. A voice whose patch it cannot run is refused at `cue`, by name. */
  readonly runs: ReadonlySet<'fn' | 'keys' | 'motion'>;
  /** Builds the mix. Everything on `Mix` is the engine's to implement. */
  create<I, O>(kit: Kit<O>, opts: MixOptions): Mix<I, O>;
}

export const mixer: Engine;   // the one that ships

mixer runs every form on the CPU, samples per subject on demand except where a channel runs as a lane (below), and is the extraction of what klieg does today. It folds each contribution into the subject's accumulator as it probes, rather than collecting every layer and merging at the end as klieg's EffectFrame does: weights are scalars that no pose depends on, so they resolve for the subject first, and a locus folds to one contribution before that contribution is merged. That order is what would let a later revision hand patches one scratch object to write into instead of returning a fresh partial per patch per subject per frame, which writes makes safe to hand out without clearing. What later engines would look like, so the seam is judged against something real and none of them is being built:

  • A baked engine runs keys voices only and emits Web Animations or CSS keyframes, so a DOM consumer like wod gets compositor-thread playback. It refuses fn and motion patches at cue.
  • A recording engine wraps another engine and captures every pose into keyframes, turning a procedural patch into data for the baked one.
  • A GPU engine writes per-subject deltas into a buffer instead of returning them, for a part count where a JS loop per frame is the cost. Same kit, same voices; probe becomes a read from the buffer.

The seam holds only if nothing outside the engine assumes probing is a function call. Two consequences for the schema: probe takes an out parameter so a buffer-backed engine can fill in place, and keys patches keep their stops on the object rather than closing over them.

Lanes

Inside mixer, a channel can run as a lane: computed for every subject at once, in Float64Arrays indexed by a number the mix gives each subject, instead of subject by subject. A host never asks for one. Lanes are on by default, and MixOptions.lanes: false turns them off, to rule one out or to compare against.

runs on a lanetakes the general path
channelsum, mul, max, and vec over one of them, as the stock functions make them, bounds or notanything else, including a custom channel claiming a stock kind

A channel is a lane only while every voice writing it qualifies, and a voice runs on lanes only while every channel it writes is one, so no patch is called twice in a frame. The mix works this out again when a voice is cued or retires, starts a fade to rest, or first keeps state; never per probe. A voice that names one subject and writes only laned channels is the exception: it joins the crowd of voices writing those channels, below, or leaves it, without the mix qualifying anything again, since a voice that fits joining laned channels and a laned voice leaving cannot move the fixed point. Such a voice also relinks only its own subject’s chain, and only a voice over every subject makes every subject meet the lanes again. A projection always reads through the general path.

  • Subjects are numbered on first sight. A subject's first probe takes the general path, where target, stagger and the fade-in origin are worked out, and gives it a number; so does each voice's first meeting with it later. drop frees the number, and for an object subject so does the host letting it go. A subject the host never probes is never numbered and costs a lane nothing.
  • The first probe after a sync fills every lane, for every numbered subject each laned voice reaches that was probed this frame or in the last frame with a probe, then copies the subject's values into the pose; later probes that frame only copy. A subject probed in neither is left out of the fill and takes the general path if it is probed before the next one, so a subject that scrolled out of view costs a lane nothing. A cue, retirement or handle change between two probes refills at the next one. So a frame costs the fill plus a probe per subject the host reads.
  • A lane rests while its subjects go unprobed. When fewer than 15% of a keys lane's subjects were probed in the last frame with a probe, or fewer than 60% of a fn or motion lane's, the lane stops filling and probes of its subjects take the general path; it fills again once that share reaches 25%, or 80%. Those lines sit either side of where filling and probing break even, measured at 1,000 and 10,000 subjects: about 19% for keys, 71–77% for a spring, and 63–100% for a stateless fn. Both paths give the same pose, so the switch only changes the cost.
  • Single-subject voices share a crowd. A laned voice whose subjects names one subject does not get a lane of its own, since it shares nothing across subjects for a lane to save: every such voice writing the same channels is a row in one crowd, in voice order. A motion voice writes one channel; a keys or stateless fn voice may write several. Crowds that share a channel take turns in voice order as a fill runs them, so a subject’s rows fold in the order the general path folds them. A row holds the per-subject numbers a lane keeps and a copy of what a fill reads of the voice (clock, rate, weight) and of its current stretch. The voice’s copy is rewritten when a handle or the mix changes the voice; the stretch’s when the patch replaces it (Motions.touched). A fill reads the crowd’s flat arrays and calls the same closed forms, and folds each row between the lanes on either side of its voice, so the order of the fold, and so its bits, are the general path’s. A row with a fade, a ramp, a change pending or a probe already this frame takes the per-subject step instead. Measured on teitou against the build before (2026-10-03, three alternated passes of bench/frame.mjs): 10k tween voices of one subject each cost 0.54–0.59 of what they did, as much as one tween voice over the same 10k subjects; 100k cost 0.31–0.33; 10k springs 0.64–0.70. Before it, the extra cost of a voice over a shared voice grew with the count, 18 ns at 1k, 48 at 10k, 226 at 100k (bench/split.mjs): the fill was bound by memory, a Voice, a Lane and a patch buffer per voice. A keys or stateless fn row reads its voice the same way and then does what a lane does for one subject: reads the stops, or calls the patch through the same function a lane calls it through. Rows join at the end as voices are cued, so they stay in voice order; a retired voice’s row is left empty until the crowd is more than half empty rows, when the next probe slides the rows left down over the empty ones, in order. Rebuilding the crowds through a full qualify instead made that one frame about 9 ms at 10k rows; sliding them keeps the worst of 12,000 churn frames near 3 ms, which is collection (teitou, 2026-10-04). Measured on teitou against the build before (2026-10-03, five alternated passes, ms a frame): 10k keys voices of one subject each by probe 1.30–1.89 from 1.60–1.77, and by pull 1.25–2.01 from 1.71–1.84, both bimodal from run to run; 10k fn voices by pull 0.93–1.18 from 1.36–1.52, and by probe 1.20–1.43 against 1.09–1.21, which a profiled run reversed. Folding each row's segment straight into the lanes, through the same search readKeys makes, rather than through a delta, took 10k such rows read by pull to 0.74 of their time and by probe to 0.85 (teitou, 2026-10-04). A row's stops are now copied as it joins into one array the crowd keeps for all its rows, phase, then value per axis, so a fill reads them in place rather than through the row's voice, its tracks and each stop's own array; the search is still the one segment makes. A row whose stops are not plain numbers on the channel's axes reads them through its voice as before. That took 10k such rows read by pull from 0.66–1.02 ms a frame to 0.34–0.36, 34–36 ns a subject against 25 a subject and voice for keys voices over every subject, and by probe from 1.00–1.37 to 0.81–0.83 (teitou, 2026-10-05). Copying the stops into each voice's own flat arrays, with the row still reaching them through its voice, had measured no different. With one of 10k tween voices stopped, its subject dropped and another cued every frame (churn), a frame costs 0.91–1.01 ms by probe and 0.54–0.62 by pull, from 9.82–9.94 and 8.73–9.07. A fn voice of one subject writing two channels, which took the general path before crowds took several channels, reads at 0.65× at 1k voices and 0.79× at 10k, and 1.05–1.11× at 100, where a fill’s bookkeeping costs more than the general path it saves (rows named, teitou, 2026-10-04). A voice naming several subjects has a lane of its own.
  • A locus runs on lanes in two passes. The general path folds a locus at its first member’s place in the voice order, and which voice that is differs by subject. So a fill first takes each locus’s members in voice order and, for each subject, sums their weights and lerps their values by share of the weight taken, as foldLocus does, with the stock channels’ own lerp; then, folding the voices in order, it folds each subject’s locus in at whichever member met that subject first. A locus’s members are on lanes all together or not at all, since a member left on the general path would count in its sum there but not on a lane; a motion member keeps the whole locus off. A blend’s signal is read by whichever member fills a subject first, and kept by subject number for the others. On teitou (2026-10-04) three voices in a locus over 10k subjects take 0.72 of the time they did on the general path, and mix.blend of three by a stateless signal 0.84.
  • sync visits only the voices with something due. Each live voice sits in a heap by the earliest mix time a sync would change it: its start, its anchored out, the end of its last pass, the end of a fade or of a subject’s ramp out. A handle change that could bring that earlier files it again. A voice whose time cannot be put ahead, one fading to rest or held and running backwards, is visited every frame. test/due.test.ts plays random mixes both ways, against a full walk, and compares states, poses and marks after every sync.
  • Lanes give the same pose, to the bit. Both paths call the same functions for phase, envelope, weight clamp, each stock channel's fold, the easing curves and the spring solver, and clamp bounds the same way after. test/lanes.test.ts runs every case both ways and compares each probe with Object.is. weightOf, atRest and a finite loop's end read what the fill keeps.
  • Two things differ, both documented on the API. A stateless patch calling setting.send from at sends every frame for every subject a lane fills, which is each subject probed that frame or the last, whether the host reads it this frame or not. A patch that first calls setting.keep partway through playing, from a call for a subject the host probed the frame before and not that frame, advances that state once more for that subject than the general path would; a patch that keeps state from its first call, or declares state, never does.
  • Motion runs on lanes as data. spring, glide and tween are their own form so a lane can sample each subject's stretch from the patch's arrays rather than calling at. A fill samples only where sampling changes nothing; a subject with a change to apply is left to the general path, so a retarget lands where it would with lanes off.

Reading in bulk. mix.pull(subjects, { position, gain }) writes each subject's values into the host's own arrays instead of a pose object. It does everything a probe does for each subject in turn, so it gives the same values, to the bit; test/lanes.test.ts runs every case through it too. While every voice is on a lane, a subject the lanes filled is copied straight from them; any other subject is folded through the general path into one scratch pose and copied from that. Measured on the fleet on 2026-10-02 (msb-uai, three passes of bench/frame.mjs), a frame read through pull costs 0.84 of one read through probe(subject, out) for three keys voices over 10,000 subjects, 0.89 at 1,000, 0.91 for a spring and 0.93 for a stateless fn. An array read again in the same order skips finding each record: the mix remembers the last array by position, with each subject’s record chain, and takes a remembered chain while the subject in that place is the same one and the chain is current, so an array edited in place stays right. That took a frame read through pull to 0.77–0.89 of what it was for a tween voice over 10,000 subjects, 0.83–0.88 for a spring and 0.83–0.85 for three keys voices (teitou, three alternated passes, 2026-10-02). Against main before those (teitou, three alternated passes, 2026-10-02), a weasel-shaped tween voice over 10,000 subjects read by pull costs 0.61–0.66 of what it did, a tween 0.59–0.65, a spring 0.68–0.74; read by probe, a tween 0.74–0.82 and a spring per subject 0.74–0.78; keys and fn rows are unchanged. Pieces from other packages

A package can publish blits pieces for a host to mix with its own: signals, patches, and the channels its patches write. Nothing compares classes, so a patch made by one copy of blits cues into a mix made by another, and a patch written against a few channels cues into a mix over more. Four rules keep that safe.

  • A published patch names its kit. patch(…, { kit }) and keys(…, { kit }) record the channels the patch was written against, and cue refuses a mix whose channel of the same name is another kind, naming both. Without it, a sum offset cued into a mix whose offset multiplies would fold wrong and say nothing. A custom channel has no kind and matches only itself, so a package that defines one exports the object for hosts to put in their kits.
  • A published patch names what it reads from the host. reads: ['pointer'], and cue refuses a mix whose host lacks the field. A patch meant for anyone reads nothing from the host.
  • A package tags its voices and drains by its tag. cue({ …, tags: ['magicsmoke'] }), then mix.drain('magicsmoke') takes that package's events and leaves every other package's queued. A tag is the vocabulary's word for a label a source attaches so a query can select voices as a set; draining is its first query.
  • A package that sends events publishes what handles them. blits mixes numbers and times events; it draws nothing. A patch that only sends is half a piece, and the other half is the package's renderer, which the host would hand the drained events. The first is @msb235/blits-quarks in packages/quarks: a patch sends { count } under a tag, and the driver drains that tag and emits the particles at the event's subject. magicsmoke still keeps its fault mix private, and the host calls smoke.cue and smoke.sync while magicsmoke syncs, probes, drains and renders inside. Exporting the patch and the renderer separately, for a host that owns the mix, is possible later and not designed.

A host adds channels of its own beside the shared ones: a kit is a plain object, so kit({ ...shared, light: vec(3, sum()), dark: max() }) is the whole mechanism, as sherpa's transform already is. Two conventions keep that safe. A host's own channel never takes a name the shared list holds, so klieg's light stays light only while the list has none. A package that names a kind of its own prefixes it with the package, klieg:light rather than light, because cue trusts two equal kinds to fold the same and nothing stops two packages claiming one bare word for different arithmetic.

The cleanest seam needs none of these: a Signal is a number per subject, so a package exporting one and a host using it as a weight agree on nothing but the subject type. Taking signals in is the same seam the other way: magicsmoke's faults take their weight as any Signal the host builds with slew, lag or level.

The klieg port

All three klieg systems move in one pass, as chosen. The port is an extraction first and a feature second, and the boundary between the two is where the baselines are checked.

  • Step one reproduced the arithmetic exactly, and is done. Timeline.poseAt is a voice per layer of each phase, weighted min(inW, outW); EffectFrame.resolve is a mix over parts with the same modulo pass and the same stagger call; the sign’s environment is a mix with one subject. mergeOffsets, addScaled, accumulate, scaleOffset and mergeEnv are all the channel kits now. klieg’s vitest suites and the Playwright baselines hold unchanged.
  • Stagger stayed klieg's, and the two are not the same operation. A voice's stagger delays a subject by milliseconds; klieg's warps the pass into (t − start) / span and clamps, so a part reaches the end of its pass with the word rather than after it. The port computes it in the patch, from setting.elapsed, and orderKey stays klieg's with it.
  • A phase is a layer, not an alternative. The three phases cue as separate voices, not as one locus: a locus folds through lerp, which is identically scaling and merging on sum but not on mul — and scale and opacity are mul channels that two phases both write inside a blend window. klieg’s own guard against three phases overlapping at once, scaling every weight by 1/Σw when the sum runs past 1, stays klieg’s for the same reason: it is a property of its phase table, not of the fold. A locus is still the right shape for alternatives that replace one another, which is what mix.blend cues.
  • Step two turns on what the extraction bought. power, kicks and dwell lose their hand-rolled frame keying, and slew takes the decay math with it: dwell's toward and kicks' drain are one function, leaving kicks its falloff and its event queue. power keeps its state machine, which is not a decay. hinge's blend mode becomes weight: signal and its stops mode becomes mix.blend; the old names stay as sugar. A host can cue an effect to a running fire and fade it in, which has no equivalent today.
  • Wrappers do not move. turns, roving, intermittent, partition and the Sequence's carry and within stay patch-level and unchanged, except that carry can be deleted once a regroup uses from: 'current'.
  • FrameCtx becomes Setting with klieg's fields on host. pointer, pointerInWord and dt keep their meanings. Every klieg patch signature changes by one property path, and a codemod does it.
  • klieg gains one runtime dependency, zero-dependency itself, ESM-only, with types.

Decided against

  • Position and rotation as one channel. A channel is the smallest thing a patch writes, so a rotation-only patch would write a zero position, and in a locus that zero pulls the other members' positions toward it by its share; apart, a locus blends each channel only among the voices that write it. Rotation could also not change its arithmetic, to quaternions say, without position. The name it was offered under, pose, is already the whole of what a mix resolves for a subject. Decided 2026-09-30.
  • A signal graph. Everything as nodes evaluated once per frame. Most general, and every existing patch would be re-expressed as nodes; a misbehaving sign would be read as a graph rather than a list. Signals stay scalars and plug in at two places.
  • Keyframes as the only form. The platform's shape and wod's. It cannot carry a hash-driven flicker or a permutation walk without an escape hatch that is the procedural form under another name. Both forms are first-class instead.
  • Adopting a tweening library. GSAP, anime.js and Motion own a property and write absolute values, so concurrent writers conflict. anime.js v4's additive mode is the closest and still has no rest rule per channel. three's AnimationMixer has the right model and is keyframe-only over object property paths.
  • Subject registration. A mix.subjects(list) call would let the mix step every state eagerly. It puts a second source of truth about the pool in the host. Lazy state keyed by identity, stepped by the gap in the timestamp, needs nothing from the host and is the rule klieg already proved.
  • A slew in place of the band. Smoothing a gate's input instead of giving it two edges. It delays the crossing without deciding it, so an input that rests on the threshold chatters anyway, and the rate needed to help at all costs visible lag.
  • Normalizing a locus's weights per channel. The other way to make a crossfade hold its value on mul: keep the scale-and-merge fold and pick a per-channel divisor. It costs the property the mixer's fold order rests on — that weights are scalars resolved for the subject before any channel is touched — and it has no answer for last, which has no arithmetic to normalize. The lerp fold gets the same result from the rule the package already has.
  • Removing a frozen voice once another covers it. WAAPI drops a finished, filling animation when a later one replaces every property it sets. In a mix, “covers” depends on weights, loci and each channel’s fold, and a voice leaving on its own would change the pose with no call to point at. The host fades it.
  • A mix option for the lurch guard. A global "normalize when over 1" would apply to effects that legitimately stack. The cap belongs to the voices that crossfade, so it is a locus.

Tests

These are the package's suite, in test/. Everything below the klieg extraction is written and green.

  • Channel laws, property-tested per stock channel over a deterministic sample: merge(v, rest) = v, scale(v, 1) = v, scale(v, 0) = rest, lerp(a, b, 0) = a, lerp(a, b, 1) = b. Each holds on the channel's own domain — max's rest is an identity for values at or above it, which is what dark and aperture are.
  • One frame, one answer. A stateful patch probed 12 times at one timestamp steps once; a second sync with the same timestamp is a no-op.
  • Catch-up. A subject unsampled for three frames steps by the whole gap on the fourth.
  • Fade continuity. A voice stopped mid-pass with a fade produces no frame-to-frame jump larger than its per-frame ramp, on every channel with rest.
  • Retarget continuity. A from: 'current' voice cued on a moving subject has a first pose equal to the subject's previous pose.
  • Band quiet. A signal held inside a gate's band for a whole pass produces no switch, whatever it does inside it. Same for a rest-less channel's contribution under a weight that wanders between off and on.
  • Locus fold. Two voices in a locus driving the same value produce that value, on every stock channel — the invariant the old cap met on sum alone. At unequal values they produce the channel's lerp at their weight ratio, which on sum is the number scaling and merging already gives and is what keeps klieg's baselines green. A locus whose weights sum below 1 scales the folded value toward rest by the sum; above 1 it caps at 1.
  • Handover. fade({ at: 'rest' }) removes per subject at the first resting frame, and at the deadline for a patch that never rests.
  • Declared writes. A patch's at sets no key outside its writes, on every subject and every pass. A voice whose patch writes a channel the kit lacks throws at cue naming both.
  • Reading another time. A projection ahead gives what the mix probes when it gets there, and moves nothing: a mix read ahead every frame plays exactly as one never read. A read back under stepMs equals the pose the mix showed at every frame, through fades, rate and weight changes, seeks and voices that have left, and a round trip back and forth repeats it. History stays inside its horizon.
  • Seeking. After seek(t) back under stepMs, with lanes and without, and through a change of the mix's rate, the mix plays on equal to what it showed at every frame, the host's recorded calls made again: cues, handle writes, fades, voice seeks, spring retargets and pushes, owners and their children, across two seeks before a subject is read again, and forward by a seek as by a sync. A call made after going back plays the new future and keeps the old one, which switchBranch plays again exactly. The suite runs on weasel-history's own History, a dev dependency. Handles held across a seek work, and done and played start over; events and hits after t come again and none at or before it does. sync refuses an earlier timestamp short of a rebase, and project takes mix time, which a rebase leaves where it was.
  • Placement. astv's orb, landing text and tint, each anchored on the one before, list their marks and play to them; a waiting voice follows a target faded early; a placement that waits on itself is refused.
  • Lanes off against lanes on. Every qualifying case runs with lanes: false and without, and each probe's channels must agree under Object.is: every patch form, stagger, target, subjects, loops, rate, ramp, seek, fades, bounds, a spring retargeted and pushed mid-flight, and a channel moving between a lane and the general path mid-animation. Subject numbers are freed and reused without showing an old subject's values.
  • Engine refusal. An engine whose runs lacks fn throws at cue naming the voice.
  • The klieg extraction is judged by klieg's own suites and the Playwright baselines, unchanged.
