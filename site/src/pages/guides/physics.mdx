# Physics with Rapier

<p class="lede">blits doesn't simulate anything. <code>@msb235/blits-rapier</code> keeps a <a href="https://rapier.rs">Rapier</a> world inside a mix, so physics plays on the mix's clock: voices push bodies, each body's pose blends with authored motion, and a seek or a debugger's scrub replays the world exactly.</p>

Click the canvas to send a gust through the boxes. **Tidy** crossfades every box from where physics has it to a place in a row; **Let go** hands them back.

## Bodies are subjects

`physics` makes the mix and the world. `sim.body` adds a body from Rapier's own descriptions, and returns the mix's subject for its pose: `position` and `rotation`, read with [`probe`](Mix.probe) like any other subject. Call `sim.sync(now)` each frame where you would call [`sync`](Mix.sync); it syncs the mix, reads what is pushing each body, and steps the world, one Rapier step per [`stepMs`](MixOptions.stepMs).

## Voices push

A body's `controls` is a second subject, with `force` and `torque` channels. `body.drive` cues a voice there, and the voice's weight scales its push, so a gust that fades in and out is a patch with a weight like any other. The gust above is one pass of a sine, cued on every box at each click; when its pass ends the voice leaves the mix.

## The simulation is a voice

Each body's pose comes from a voice, `body.simulated`, that reads the body from the world. It plays in the role `sim.role`, and voices in one [role](../../role/) are alternatives: cue an authored pose in that role and the two crossfade by their weights. The demo hands the boxes over with one [`level`](../../signal/#api-level), eased by [`lag`](../../signal/#api-lag): `physics` takes a `weight` signal for every simulated voice, and the tidy voices weigh the other [`share`](../../signal/#api-share) of the same signal, so the frame loop only syncs and draws. A voice cued outside the role adds on top of wherever physics has the body instead, for a wobble or an offset.

## Seeking

The world lives in the mix as the state of a stateful patch, so a mix with [`history`](MixOptions.history) and a tape keeps copies of it and replays from the nearest one on a [`seek`](Mix.seek). The replay lands on the same poses as the first pass. Impulses and dropped bodies are logged at their mix time and happen again when a replay passes them; a change made to the Rapier world directly does not survive a seek.

The package's [README](https://github.com/orochi235/blits/tree/main/packages/rapier#readme) lists the channels in 2D and 3D and what it does not do yet.
